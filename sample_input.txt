The music department is preparing an inventory of its keyboard instruments.

The first instrument is a Yamaha P-150, manufactured by Yamaha. Its specification
sheet lists 88 keys, a width of 1385 mm, a height of 159 mm, and a weight of
33.7 kg. The same sheet states that the Yamaha P-150 has 32-note polyphony and
two loudspeakers. A technician measured this particular Yamaha P-150 and recorded
its width as 138.5 cm. Both measurements have been retained in the inventory.
The technician described its finish as black.

The second instrument is a Roland JUNO-106, manufactured by Roland. The Roland
JUNO-106 has 61 keys and six-voice polyphony. Its accompanying inventory card
records a width of 106 cm and a weight of 10 kg. A separate handwritten note
claims that the same Roland JUNO-106 weighs 11 kg. The department has not yet
checked which weight is correct. Its height was not recorded.

A Yamaha DX7 is stored beside the Roland JUNO-106. Yamaha manufactured the
Yamaha DX7. The Yamaha DX7 has 61 keys, a width of 101.8 cm, and a weight of
14.2 kg. It provides 16-note polyphony. The Yamaha DX7 is a different instrument
from the Yamaha DX7II-D listed in the next entry.

The Yamaha DX7II-D is also manufactured by Yamaha and has 61 keys. Its inventory
record does not state its width, height, or weight. Staff must not copy those
measurements from the Yamaha DX7 because the two entries refer to different
models.

The department also owns a Nord Stage 4 88 manufactured by Clavia DMI. The
Nord Stage 4 88 has 88 keys and a width of 1282 mm. Its weight is recorded as
19.6 kg. The transport case used for this instrument weighs 8 kg, but that figure
describes the case rather than the keyboard. The case's external width is
1420 mm and must not be recorded as the instrument's width.

Another entry describes a Korg SV-2 88 manufactured by Korg. The Korg SV-2 88
has 88 keys, a width of 1356 mm, and a height of 157 mm. Its recorded weight is
21.45 kg. In a later maintenance entry, the Korg SV-2 88 is mentioned again:
the technician confirmed that this instrument still has its original 88-key
keyboard. No new measurement of its width was taken.

The Yamaha CK61 is manufactured by Yamaha. Its product card lists 61 keys,
a width of 910 mm, a height of 109 mm, and a weight of 5.6 kg. The Yamaha CK61
has two loudspeakers. The supplier offered a bundle containing the Yamaha CK61,
a stand, and a carrying bag with a combined shipping weight of 12.4 kg. That
shipping weight is for the complete package, not the Yamaha CK61 alone.

The Yamaha CK88 is a separate model manufactured by Yamaha. It has 88 keys.
The inventory notes that the Yamaha CK61 and Yamaha CK88 were ordered together,
but only the Yamaha CK61's dimensions were entered. No dimensions or weight are
given here for the Yamaha CK88.

The Roland FP-10 is manufactured by Roland and has 88 keys. The Roland FP-10
is described as black. Its inventory sheet records a width of 1284 mm and a
weight of 12.3 kg. A maintenance report later refers to this same instrument
as "the FP-10" and records that its height is 140 mm.

A prototype instrument is listed as the Aurora Testboard X9, manufactured by
Aurora Instruments. The Aurora Testboard X9 has 73 keys, measures 1100 mm in
width, and weighs 9.2 kg. Neither the model name nor the manufacturer has been
verified against the department's knowledge base.

Finally, the storeroom contains a 104-key USB computer keyboard, a sustain
pedal weighing 0.7 kg, and an equipment rack that is 180 cm tall. These are
accessories or other equipment, not musical keyboard instruments. A colleague
remarked that "the newer one is lighter," but did not identify which instruments
were being compared.
